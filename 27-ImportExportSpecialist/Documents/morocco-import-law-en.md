# CHAPTER 85 

## MACHINES, APPARATUS AND ELECTRICAL MATERIALS AND THEIR PARTS; SOUND RECORDING OR REPRODUCTION APPARATUS, IMAGE AND SOUND RECORDING OR REPRODUCTION APPARATUS FOR TELEVISION, AND PARTS AND ACCESSORIES OF THESE APPARATUSES

## Notes.

1.- The following are excluded from this Chapter:
a) electrically heated blankets, cushions, sleeping bags, and similar articles; clothing, shoes, ear warmers, and other electrically heated items worn on the body;
b) glass articles of $n^{\circ} 70.11$;
c) machines and apparatus of $n^{\circ} 84.86$;
d) vacuum cleaners of the types used in medicine, surgery, or for dental or veterinary arts ($n^{\circ} 90.18$);
e) electrically heated furniture of Chapter 94.  
2.- Items that may fall under $n^{\circ}s 85.01 to 85.04$, on one hand, and $n^{\circ}s 85.11, 85.12, 85.40, 85.41$, or 85.42, on the other hand, are classified in those last five positions.

However, mercury vapor mutators with a metal tank are included under $n^{\circ} 85.04$.  
3.- For the purposes of $n^{\circ} 85.07$, the term electric accumulators also includes accumulators presented with auxiliary elements that contribute to the energy storage and supply function fulfilled by the accumulator or are intended to protect it from potential damage, such as electrical connectors, temperature control devices (thermistors, for example), and circuit protection devices. They may also include part of the protective envelope of the devices they are destined for.  
4.- $n^{\circ} 85.09$ covers, provided that they are electromechanical devices of types commonly used for domestic purposes:
a) floor polishers, food grinders, and mixers, fruit juices and vegetable presses, of all weights;
b) other devices with a maximum weight of 20 kg, excluding fans and exhaust hoods with built-in fan extraction or recycling, even filtered ($n^{\circ} 84.14$), centrifugal laundry dryers ($n^{\circ} 84.21$), dishwashing machines ($n^{\circ} 84.22$), washing machines ($n^{\circ} 84.50$), ironing machines ($n^{\circ}s 84.20 or 84.51, depending on whether they are calenders or not), sewing machines ($n^{\circ} 84.52$), electric scissors ($n^{\circ} 84.67$), and electrothermal devices ($n^{\circ} 85.16$).  
5.- For the purposes of $n^{\circ} 85.17$, smart phones are defined as cell phones equipped with an operating system designed to ensure the functions of an automatic data processing machine, such as downloading and operating multiple applications simultaneously, including third-party applications, and even equipped with other features such as a digital camera or navigation system.  
6.- For the purposes of $n^{\circ} 85.23$:
a) permanent data storage devices based on semiconductors ("flash memory cards" or "flash electronic memory cards" for example) are defined as storage devices that have a connection plug, incorporating, within a single envelope, one or more flash memories ("FLASH E-PROM", for example), in the form of integrated circuits mounted on a printed circuit board. They may include a controller in the form of an integrated circuit and discrete passive components such as capacitors and resistors;
b) the term smart cards refers to cards that contain, embedded in their mass, one or more electronic integrated circuits (a microprocessor, static RAM (SRAM) or ROM) in the form of chips. These cards may be equipped with contacts, a magnetic strip, or a built-in antenna but do not contain any other active or passive circuit elements.  
7.- For the purposes of $n^{\circ} 85.24$, a flat panel display module is defined as devices or apparatus intended to display information, equipped at a minimum with a display screen, designed to be incorporated into articles falling under other positions before their use. The display screens of flat panel display modules can, notably but not exclusively, be flat, curved, flexible, foldable, or expandable.  
Flat panel display modules may incorporate additional elements, including those necessary for receiving video signals and distributing those signals into pixels on the screen. However, $n^{\circ} 85.24$ does not include display modules that have components intended to convert video signals (for example, a scaler integrated circuit, a decoder integrated circuit, or an application processor) or that have taken on the character of goods of other positions. For the purpose of classifying the flat panel display modules defined in this Note, $n^{\circ} 85.24$ takes precedence over any other position in the nomenclature.  
8.- For the purposes of $n^{\circ} 85.34$, printed circuits are defined as circuits obtained by placing conductive elements, contacts, or other printed components (inductors, resistors, capacitors, for example) on an insulating support, using any printing method (embedding, electroforming, etching, etc.) or by the so-called "layer" circuit technology, alone or combined according to a pre-established scheme, excluding any element capable of producing, rectifying, modulating, or amplifying an electrical signal (semiconductor elements, for example). The term printed circuits does not cover circuits combined with elements other than those obtained during the printing process, nor discrete resistors, capacitors, or inductors.

However, printed circuits may be equipped with non-printed connection elements. Layer circuits (thin or thick) containing active and passive elements obtained during the same technological process are classified under $n^{\circ} 85.42$.  
9.- For the purposes of $n^{\circ} 85.36$, fiber optic connectors, bundles or cables are defined as connectors that merely serve to mechanically align the optical fibers end-to-end in a line digital system. They do not serve any other function such as amplification, regeneration, or modification of a signal.  
10.- $n^{\circ} 85.37$ does not include wireless infrared devices for remote control of television receivers or other electrical devices ($n^{\circ} 85.43$).  
11.- For the purposes of $n^{\circ} 85.39, the term light sources with light-emitting diodes (LEDs) covers:
a) LED modules, which are electrical light sources based on LEDs, arranged in electric circuits and containing electrical, mechanical, thermal, or optical elements. They also contain discrete active or passive elements or articles of $n^{\circ}s 85.36 or 85.42 for the purpose of supplying power or controlling power. LED modules do not have a base designed to be easily installed or replaced in a luminaire and allow electrical contact and mechanical fixing.
b) lamps and tubes with light-emitting diodes (LEDs), which are electrical light sources composed of one or more LED modules, containing other elements such as electrical, mechanical, thermal, or optical elements. They differ from LED modules by their base designed to be easily installed or replaced in a luminaire and for allowing electrical contact and mechanical fixing.  
12.- For the purposes of $n^{\circ}s 85.41 and 85.42, the following are considered as:
a) $1^{\circ}$) Semiconductor devices are devices whose operation depends on the variation of resistivity under the influence of an electric field or semiconductor transducers.  
Semiconductor devices may also include an assembly of several elements, even equipped with active or passive devices whose function is auxiliary.  
For the purposes of this definition, semiconductor transducers are semiconductor sensors, semiconductor actuators, semiconductor resonators, and semiconductor oscillators, which are types of discrete semiconductor devices that perform an intrinsic function that can convert any type of physical or chemical phenomenon or action into an electrical signal or convert an electrical signal into any type of physical phenomenon or action.  
All components of a transducer are combined in a practically inseparable manner and may also include inseparable materials required for constructing or operating a semiconductor device.  
For the purposes of this definition:
1) The term semiconductor means constructed or manufactured on a semiconductor substrate or consisting of semiconductor-based materials manufactured using semiconductor technology, in which the substrate or semiconductor materials play a critical and irreplaceable role in the function and performance of the transducer and whose operation is based on semiconductor, physical, electrical, chemical, and optical properties.  
2) Physical or chemical phenomena relate to phenomena such as pressure, sound waves, acceleration, vibration, motion, orientation, stress, intensity of magnetic field, intensity of electric field, light, radioactivity, humidity, creep, concentration of chemicals, etc.  
3) Semiconductor sensors are a type of semiconductor devices consisting of microelectronic or mechanical structures that are created in the mass or on the surface of a semiconductor and whose function is to detect physical or chemical quantities and convert them into electrical signals produced by resulting variations of electrical properties or mechanical structure deformation.  
4) Semiconductor actuators are a type of semiconductor devices consisting of microelectronic and mechanical structures that are created in the mass or on the surface of a semiconductor and whose function is to convert electrical signals into physical motion.  
5) Semiconductor resonators are a type of semiconductor devices consisting of microelectronic and mechanical structures that are created in the mass or on the surface of a semiconductor and whose function is to generate a mechanical or electrical oscillation of a predefined frequency that depends on the physical geometry of these structures in response to an external electrical signal.  
6) Semiconductor oscillators are a type of semiconductor devices consisting of microelectronic or mechanical structures that are created in the mass or on the surface of a semiconductor and whose function is to generate a mechanical or electrical oscillation of a predefined frequency that depends on the physical geometry of these structures.  
$2^{\circ}$) Light-emitting diodes (LEDs) are semiconductor devices made from semiconductor materials that transform electrical energy into visible, infrared, or ultraviolet radiation, even when electrically connected together and even combined with protection diodes. LEDs of $n^{\circ} 85.41 do not contain elements intended to supply power or control power.  
b) Integrated circuits:
$1^{\circ}$) Monolithic integrated circuits in which the circuit elements (diodes, transistors, resistors, capacitors, inductors, etc.) are created in the mass (essentially) and at the surface of a semiconductor material (for example, doped silicon, gallium arsenide, silicon-germanium, indium phosphide), forming an inseparable whole;  
$2^{\circ}$) Hybrid integrated circuits that bring together, practically inseparably, by interconnections or linking wires, on the same insulating substrate (glass, ceramic, etc.) passive elements (resistors, capacitors, inductors, etc.), obtained through thin or thick layer circuit technology, and active elements (diodes, transistors, monolithic integrated circuits, etc.) obtained via semiconductor technology. These circuits may also include discrete components;  
$3^{\circ}$) Multi-chip integrated circuits consisting of two or more interconnected monolithic integrated circuits, combined in a practically inseparable manner, resting on one or more insulating substrates or not and containing or not pins, but without other active or passive circuit elements.  
$4^{\circ}$) Multi-component integrated circuits, which are combinations of one or more monolithic, hybrid, or multi-chip integrated circuits and including at least one of the following components: sensors, actuators, silicon resonators, even combined among themselves, or components ensuring the functions of products that may fall under $n^{\circ}s 85.32, 85.33, 85.41$, or inductors that may fall under $n^{\circ} 85.04$, and which are assembled in a practically inseparable manner into a single body such as an integrated circuit, to form a component of the type used to be assembled on a printed circuit board or another support by connecting pins, leads, knobs, pads, bumps, or discs.  
For the purposes of this definition:
1. Components may be discrete, made independently of one another, and then assembled into a multi-component integrated circuit or integrated with other components.  
2. The expression silicon means that the component is made on a silicon substrate or made of silicon-based materials or made on an integrated circuit chip.  
3. a) Silicon sensors consist of microelectronic or mechanical structures that are created in the mass or on the surface of a semiconductor and whose function is to detect physical or chemical phenomena and convert them into electrical signals when variations of electrical properties or mechanical structure deformation occur. Physical or chemical phenomena relate to phenomena such as pressure, sound waves, acceleration, vibration, motion, orientation, stress, intensity of magnetic field, intensity of electric field, light, radioactivity, humidity, creep, chemical concentrations, etc.  
b) Silicon actuators consist of microelectronic and mechanical structures that are created in the mass or on the surface of a semiconductor and whose function is to convert electrical signals into physical movement.  
c) Silicon resonators are components that consist of microelectronic or mechanical structures that are created in the mass or on the surface of a semiconductor and whose function is to generate a mechanical or electrical oscillation of a predefined frequency that depends on the physical geometry of these structures in response to an external signal.  
d) Silicon oscillators are active components consisting of microelectronic or mechanical structures that are created in the mass or on the surface of a semiconductor and whose function is to generate a mechanical or electrical oscillation of a predefined frequency that depends on the physical geometry of these structures.  
For the purposes of classifying the articles defined in this Note, $n^{\circ}s 85.41 and 85.42 take precedence over any other position in the Nomenclature, except for $n^{\circ} 85.23 that may cover them due to their function.  
12.- For the purposes of $n^{\circ} 85.48, the term batteries and battery packs includes those that have become unusable as such due to breakage, cutting, wear, or other reasons or that are not capable of being recharged.

# Sub-position Notes.

1.     - $n^{\circ} 8525.81 only includes television cameras, digital cameras, and ultra-high-speed camcorders that have one or more of the following characteristics:

- recording speed greater than $0.5 \mathrm{~mm}$ per microsecond;
- time resolution of 50 nanoseconds or less;
- frame rate exceeding 225,000 images per second.

2- regarding $n^{\circ} 8525.82$, radiation-resistant cameras are designed or shielded to operate in environments subjected to high radiation levels. These cameras are designed to withstand a total radiation dose of more than $50 \times 10^{3} \mathrm{~Gy}$ (silicon) ($5 \times 10^{6} \mathrm{rad}$ (silicon)) without their operation being affected.

3- Sub-position 8525.83 covers television cameras, digital cameras, and night vision camcorders that use a photocathode to convert available natural light into electrons that can be amplified and converted to produce a visible image. This sub-position excludes thermal imaging cameras ($n^{\circ} 8525.89$, generally).  
4.- $n^{\circ} 8527.12 only covers radio cassettes with built-in amplifier, without built-in speaker, capable of operating without an external electrical power source and whose dimensions do not exceed 170 mm x $100 \mathrm{~mm} \times 45 \mathrm{~mm}$.
5.- For the purposes of $n^{\circ}s 8549.11 to 8549.19, the term waste and scrap of waste electrical and electronic equipment includes those which have become unusable as such due to breakage, cutting, wear, or other reasons or which are not capable of being recharged.
